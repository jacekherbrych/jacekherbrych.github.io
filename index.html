I'm a theoretical physicist studying how interactions between particles give rise to collective phenomena in quantum many-body systems. My research focuses on low-dimensional systems, with interests spanning quantum magnetism, transport, nonequilibrium dynamics, and unconventional superconductivity. I combine analytical approaches with numerical many-body methods to understand the mechanisms underlying these phenomena. Since 2019, I have been based at the Institute of Theoretical Physics at Wrocław University of Science and Technology, Poland. Previously, I held research positions in Slovenia, Greece, and the USA.

◦ research interests

⚬ Strongly correlated multi-orbital systems
The collective behavior of interacting electrons gives rise to a wide range of phenomena, including magnetism, metal-insulator transitions, and unconventional superconductivity. In materials with several active electronic orbitals, the interplay between charge, spin, and orbital degrees of freedom creates particularly rich physics. Understanding how these ingredients cooperate or compete is a central challenge in condensed-matter theory.

Our group studies the equilibrium properties and dynamics of strongly correlated systems, with particular emphasis on low-dimensional models. We investigate how electronic interactions generate magnetic correlations, influence the motion and pairing of charge carriers, and produce phases with nontrivial topological properties. Combining numerical many-body methods with analytical approaches, we aim to identify the microscopic mechanisms underlying these collective phenomena and connect simplified theoretical models with the properties of quantum materials.

Nat. Commun. 12, 2955 (2021)
Nat. Commun. 14, 8524 (2023)
Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 068001 (2025)

⚬ Transport properties in low-dimensional quantum systems
How do spin, charge, and energy move through a quantum system? How does an interacting system relax after a perturbation, and what determines whether it reaches thermal equilibrium? These questions lie at the intersection of condensed-matter physics and statistical mechanics. They are especially rich in low dimensions, where quantum fluctuations, interactions, and conservation laws can strongly influence the dynamics.

We investigate transport and relaxation in quantum many-body systems, focusing on the roles of interactions, disorder, and dynamical constraints. Our interests range from ballistic propagation and diffusion to slow relaxation and the persistence of memory of an initial state. We study both isolated systems and systems coupled to an environment, exploring how microscopic processes give rise to large-scale transport and how equilibrium properties are related to nonequilibrium evolution.

Phys. Rev. B 105, L081105 (2022)
Phys. Rev. B 107, 045134 (2023)
Phys. Rev. B 112, 195116 (2025)

⚬ Dynamics of quantum circuits
Quantum circuits offer a complementary way to study the dynamics of interacting quantum systems. Built from sequences of local operations, they provide a flexible setting for investigating how entanglement develops, how information spreads, and how collective behavior emerges. Measurements and coupling to an environment introduce further possibilities, allowing coherent quantum evolution and irreversible processes to be studied within a common framework.

We are interested in how the interplay between quantum operations, measurements, and conservation laws shapes many-body dynamics. Our broader aim is to connect ideas from quantum information with questions about thermalization, transport, and collective phenomena, using quantum circuits to explore general principles of nonequilibrium quantum physics.
